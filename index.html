THE REAL PROBLEM

You're not missing ideas.
You're missing a system.

Collecting tools feels like progress and is not. Here is where most people actually get stuck.

01

Twelve tools, three used badly

You subscribe to everything in month one, use almost none of it properly, and conclude AI is overhyped.

02

No idea which business to start

Service, product, SaaS, audience — each is individually cheap to begin, so you start three and learn nothing from any.

03

You build things nobody buys

Months in private, then a polished launch to silence. The validation step was skipped because it felt uncomfortable.

04

Marketing feels like shouting

Distribution — not the product — is almost always the real problem, and nobody taught you a repeatable way to do it alone.

05

No dated roadmap

Motivation has a half-life. Without a deadline and a week-by-week sequence, the excited version of you disappears by month four.

The gap is almost never knowledge. It's the willingness to take one small, uncomfortable action within twenty-four hours.

— Chapter 1, page 11

CHAPTER 12

Six founders, six paths — including the slow parts

Every story is presented with its timeline, because compressing them into "built a business, sold it for millions" is the most misleading thing about how they're usually told.

Maor Shlomo · Base44

$80M acquisition in ~16 months

Incorporated January 2025. Up to thirteen production updates a day, feedback from friends rather than marketing, growth by word of mouth. Acquired by Wix for $80 million in cash, with no co-founder and no employees.

The uncomfortable detail: he was effectively on call around the clock during the growth period.

Pieter Levels · Photo AI

~$132K MRR, 87%+ net margin

More than seventy launched projects behind him. Launch fast, charge from day one, automate aggressively, build in public, treat most projects as disposable experiments.

The uncomfortable detail: the seventy failed projects are the actual story.

Justin Welsh · Courses + newsletter

$10M+ cumulative

Started publishing on LinkedIn in 2019. Twenty-nine months to the first $1 million cumulative; past $10 million by 2025, with no full-time employees and no paid advertising at any point.

The uncomfortable detail: nearly two and a half years of daily publishing before the first million.

Kat Norton · Miss Excel

$2.9M/year

Corporate finance in 2020, then Excel tutorials on TikTok, then courses. Bootstrapped, sole operator through the period it first crossed a million in course sales.

The uncomfortable detail: the format suited her specifically; copying it without the personality does not reproduce the result.

Nevo David · Postiz

$145K MRR

A social scheduling platform built solo with AI coding tools, marketed through build-in-public content, grown by word of mouth and organic search rather than paid acquisition.

The uncomfortable detail: a crowded category with well-funded competitors. He won on focus, not on being first.

Dana Snyder · Nonprofit AI consulting

Hundreds of clients, 1 employee

The most instructive story here for non-technical readers. No technical background whatsoever; built the platform over roughly six months using Replit's AI coding tools.

The uncomfortable detail: "no code required" does not mean "no effort required".
